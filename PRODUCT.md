# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary users are learners aged 15 and older, especially secondary-school, college, and university students. Learners aged 15–17 remain guardian-supported; learners aged 18 and older should be able to manage their own accounts. Parents and younger learners remain supported where their existing workflows fit this audience and can be retained without compromising learner privacy or experience.

## Product Purpose

WiselyFox is an inclusive learning platform that helps learners study through lessons, study guides, quizzes, assessments, focus tools, and progress tracking. The redesign should make these capabilities useful and welcoming to older learners while preserving working learning and account workflows.

## Positioning

The existing product combines structured lessons and assessments with mastery-based progress and adaptive lesson recommendations. Preserve those useful learning mechanisms without presenting them as artificial intelligence.

## Operating Context

Learners use the web app to find subjects, study lessons and guides, take quizzes, use focus tools, and review progress. Parents currently manage child profiles, accessibility preferences, schedules, rewards, and progress. Tutors can support learners through a marketplace with explicit, revocable sharing of limited progress data.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Preserve core learning, assessment, progress, accessibility, safeguarding, account, and billing behavior.
- Keep useful AI-powered functionality where it serves learners, but remove AI branding, jargon, and AI-styled visual treatment from user-facing experiences.
- Retain parent and child support where it remains appropriate for the older-learner focus.
- Keep guardian-supported accounts for learners aged 15–17 and provide self-managed accounts for learners aged 18 and older.
- Gate self-managed registration with an 18+ confirmation and broad age-band selection; do not collect or store a date of birth.
- Existing learning mechanisms include spaced repetition, difficulty adjustment, study guides, quizzes, focus sessions, schedules, and adaptive recommendations.
- Learners aged 24 and older should be able to access the product, but do not claim their learning content is age-tailored: the current age-band taxonomy and curriculum stop at 23.
- Parent controls sensitive child profile data. Tutor access to learner progress is explicitly granted, pseudonymous, limited, and revocable.
- Accessibility support includes adjustable text presentation, contrast, session length, predictable layouts, low-pressure interactions, and text-to-speech-ready content.
- WiselyFox is an education tool, not a medical device. Use strengths-based language and do not imply diagnosis.
- Preserve account, consent, privacy, export, deletion, and audit behavior.

## Brand Commitments

Keep the WiselyFox name and the book theme. The user requests a more natural, age-appropriate experience for learners 15 and older, including university students, without AI-themed wording or visuals.

## Evidence on Hand

- Product and pilot documentation: `README.md`, `docs/PEDAGOGY.md`, `docs/INCLUSIVE_LEARNING_RESEARCH.md`, `docs/DATA_GOVERNANCE.md`, and `docs/FAMILY_TESTING.md`.
- The repository contains implemented learning, assessment, parent, tutor, authentication, marketing, and accessibility flows.
- No verified testimonials, learning-outcome claims, or external endorsements were identified in the product documentation; do not invent them.

## Product Principles

- Make learning tasks and progress clear without adding pressure or judgement.
- Respect learner agency while retaining appropriate family safeguards.
- Keep accessibility preferences and predictable interaction patterns available.
- Explain recommendations through helpful product language, not AI terminology.
- Preserve privacy, consent, and user control across existing workflows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Retain the existing inclusive-learning accommodations: adjustable text and contrast, manageable sessions and breaks, predictable layouts, explicit steps, low-pressure error handling, and text-to-speech-ready content. Do not present accommodations as diagnoses.
